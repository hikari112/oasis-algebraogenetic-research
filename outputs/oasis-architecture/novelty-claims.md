# OASIS minimal novelty claims

These claims identify the construction that must survive later mathematical and literature review. They are research claims, not patent claims or legal conclusions.

## Claim 1 — process-generated coordinates

A learning system whose finite features are not selected from a supplied basis, but synthesized as exact pullbacks and typed compositions of primitive probes under an infinite process action.

## Claim 2 — obstruction-directed learning

A finite process emulator is used only as an adversarial critic. Failures of multiplication or freeness generate probe-search constraints, and newly synthesized probes are chosen partly by their ability to expose the emulator's forced process aliases.

## Claim 3 — non-sofic essentiality

The internal non-sofic action is not an attached factor: removing or quotienting it changes the generated probe sequence. Formally, the complete probe algebra has observable kernel `K_A` with non-sofic quotient `Γ/K_A`.

## Claim 4 — external approximation without internal approximation

The system approximates only valuations of finite probes. Internal process composition remains an exact word operation. Finite emulators may be proposed and refuted, but never become the authoritative process representation.

## Claim 5 — exact replayable Bayesian synthesis

Each learned coordinate is stored as a seed probe plus an exact derivation word. Each external update is stored as a Bayesian or exponential-tilt operation. Prediction is reproduced from this audit log, and a mismatched derivation is rejected.

## Nontriviality test

The construction collapses to prior architectures if any of the following is true:

- probes are merely chosen from a fixed named basis;
- the non-sofic group can be removed without changing training;
- exact process words are replaced by learned finite transitions;
- the observable quotient is sofic; or
- obstruction witnesses do not alter probe synthesis under any finite budget.

The finite prototype passes the first, third, and fifth distinctions and shows a measurable version of the second under constrained probe budgets. The fourth distinction remains the central infinite theorem.
