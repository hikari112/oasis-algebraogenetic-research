# Endogenous expressibility: frontier realization stress test

## Status

This note is a constructive stress test written after the 1000-kilometre
reset. It is not a proof, a literature classification, a novelty claim, or a
route to any headline conjecture. Its purpose is to ask what the refreshed
foundation would have to mean *inside* five different native subjects before
any analogy is allowed to count.

The proposed common layer is deliberately weak:

> A finite interaction may change which tests, types, or interventions are
> operationally available. A global counterexample may therefore be an
> infinite strategy that survives every test born from its previous answers.

The conjectures do not share their tests, costs, compactness theorems, or
effectivity bridges. If those cannot be supplied natively, the common layer is
only philosophy.

## 1. The common interface

For a native problem class \(\mathcal P\), a causal formation instance should
provide at least:

1. a rooted category or event structure of finite histories \(h\);
2. a local language \(\mathcal L_h\) of currently admissible questions;
3. answer types \(A_h(q)\) and a continuation operation
   \((h,q,a)\mapsto h'\);
4. birth certificates explaining why an object of
   \(\mathcal L_{h'}\) was not merely selected from \(\mathcal L_h\);
5. a native cost on histories, questions, answers, and coherent strategies;
6. a presentation-invariant equivalence that preserves question birth rather
   than only completed traces; and
7. a realization map from formal question birth to a valid operation in the
   native subject.

No frontier section below yet supplies items 6 and 7. Each section is a list
of native obligations for a future realization, not a realization itself.

There are three different levels of growth:

- **Level A: born address.** An answer supplies the parameter or location of
  the next query inside a fixed query type. This is ordinary adaptivity.
- **Level B: born probe.** A certified failure constructs a new predicate,
  functional, or representation inside a fixed meta-grammar, with a
  presentation-invariant initial or minimal admission role and a separation
  from a declared bounded old-observer class.
- **Level C: born former.** The interaction creates a new object-language type
  or formation rule, retained with its universal birth role, not induced by
  generator adjunction in the old metagrammar, not conservatively translatable
  back, preserved by genetic equivalence, and essentially used later.

The oracle iteration \(x\mapsto f(x)\mapsto f^2(x)\) is an exact Level-A
calibration. It is not evidence for Levels B or C.

### 1.1 Counterexample--resolver game

For each proposed bad object \(z\), imagine a game:

\[
\text{native defect}
\longrightarrow
\text{admissible diagnostic}
\longrightarrow
\text{native response}
\longrightarrow
\text{refined defect}.
\tag{1}
\]

A finite play is not a finite approximation to a solution unless the native
subject proves that interpretation. A global good object should not be
identified merely with the existence of responses to every finite play. The
stronger question is whether one bounded, causally coherent strategy answers
all legal plays.

When the native cost has a declared scalarization in
\([0,\infty]\), define provisionally

\[
\kappa_z(n)
=
\inf\{\operatorname{Cost}(\sigma):
\sigma\text{ answers every legal play from }z
\text{ of causal depth at most }n\}.
\tag{2}
\]

The numbers in (2) are not comparable across subjects. If cost is vector- or
order-valued, replace the infimum by its minimal attainable elements or Pareto
frontier, or declare the upward-closed set of feasible upper bounds; do not
silently scalarize it. Only
structural properties such as boundedness, coercive growth, or failure of
compactness may be compared after native realization.

### 1.2 Strategic compactness schema

Pointwise replies do not suffice. Fix one native bound \(B<\infty\). A valid
common dream can only have the form of a theorem *schema* such as:

\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{for every depth }n\text{ there is a nonempty compact Hausdorff space }S_n(B)\\
\text{of coherent strategies on all legal plays through depth }n,\\
\text{all using the same bound }B,\text{ with continuous coherent restrictions}\\
S_{n+1}(B)\longrightarrow S_n(B)
\\[2mm]
\Longrightarrow
\varprojlim_n S_n(B)\ne\varnothing.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]

The conclusion gives one global \(B\)-bounded coherent strategy. It must still
be connected by a separate native effectivity theorem to the desired object.
Every line of (3) changes meaning from one frontier to
another. Omitting any line turns local solvability into an invalid global
conclusion.

## 2. Hodge realization requirements

### Native histories

A baseline instance must fix a smooth projective complex variety \(X\), a
codimension \(p\), and
\(\alpha\in H^{2p}(X,\mathbf Q)\cap H^{p,p}(X)\), together with the rational
cycle group and cycle-class map under discussion. A history must then live
inside algebraic geometry: restrictions to algebraic subvarieties, algebraic
correspondences, specializations or degenerations, partial cycle candidates,
and certificates produced from those operations.

### Native questions and answers

Questions might involve restriction, coniveau, periods, Abel--Jacobi or normal
function data, variation in an algebraic family, or compatibility of partial
cycle constructions. Answers must be algebraic-geometric objects or exact
certificates; a numerical approximation to a period is not a native answer.

### Possible birth mechanism

Failure of a partial cycle construction under restriction or specialization
could select a new correspondence or incidence condition. If this only
chooses a new parameter in a predeclared family, it is Level A. A Level-B
event would construct a new functorial algebraic probe from the failed lift.
A Level-C event would require a new algebraic formation principle and is much
harder to type without smuggling it into the meta-language.

### Native cost and counterexample cascade

Possible costs include cycle degree, field of definition, codimension,
complexity of correspondences, and depth of specialization. Under a sound,
continuation-complete, and cost-coercive realization of the game, a
hypothetical nonalgebraic rational Hodge class would have to survive an
infinite coherent sequence of generated algebraic diagnostics while forcing
unbounded native complexity. No such realization is supplied here.

### Mandatory effectivity bridge

Bounded termination must produce an actual rational algebraic cycle whose
cycle class is the given Hodge class. Density, analytic limits, local cycles,
or membership in a completed cycle space do not suffice.

### Controls and kill tests

- Divisor classes and the Lefschetz \((1,1)\) theorem are the first positive
  control.
- Curves, products with explicitly controlled cycles, and functorial pullback
  or pushforward are basic descent controls.
- Kill the program if every generated question remains purely cohomological
  and no operation constructs algebraic cycle data.
- Kill any claimed proof that replaces rational algebraicity by dense
  approximation or unbounded limiting degree.

## 3. Three-dimensional Navier--Stokes realization requirements

### Native histories

A baseline must fix the domain (for example \(\mathbf T^3\)), viscosity,
forcing and boundary conventions, a smooth divergence-free initial-data
class, and the exact classical or weak solution notion. A history then
consists of equation-derived local information: local-energy or suitable
weak-solution data, parabolic cylinders, frequency
localizations, pressure decompositions, rescalings, and exact inequalities.
Numerical grids are not automatically finite exact histories of a smooth
solution.

### Native questions and answers

Questions may be local energy tests, solution-adapted cylinders, frequency
packets, vortex-stretching commutators, pressure-transport probes, or blow-up
profiles. Answers are exact bounds or defects derived from the equations.

### Possible birth mechanism

Concentration can create the address and scale of the next diagnostic. That is
usually Level A. The stronger target is a failure that constructs a new
coercive probe or multiplier with a universal equation-derived role, rather
than selecting another member of a known test-function dictionary.

### Native cost and counterexample cascade

Cost must track scale, frequency, concentration, energy, enstrophy, pressure,
and dissipation with the correct Navier--Stokes scaling. Under a sound and
continuation-complete diagnostic game, a blow-up would induce an infinite
critical play in which every local repair exposes a finer defect; the converse
requires its own theorem.

### Mandatory effectivity bridge

The framework must derive a coercive estimate that prevents the critical
cascade or proves continuation. Weak existence, finite energy alone, adaptive
meshing, or a formally generated test atlas does not establish regularity.

### Controls and kill tests

- For linear Stokes evolution, the **birth extension** should be equivalent to
  the identity: ordinary evolution and Level-A adaptive addresses may change,
  but no nonlinear Level-B/C doctrine growth should be hallucinated there.
- Two-dimensional Navier--Stokes is the first global-control case.
- Hyperdissipative or otherwise subcritical models test whether the native
  cost detects known coercivity.
- Kill the program if its generated tests are only renamed epsilon-regularity,
  concentration compactness, or adaptive discretization without a new exact
  estimate.
- Kill any flux identity that is not derived from the PDE or has the wrong
  scaling.

## 4. Collatz realization requirements

### Native histories

Fix the ordinary map
\(T(n)=n/2\) for even \(n\) and \(T(n)=3n+1\) for odd \(n\); an accelerated
variant is a different realization and must not be substituted silently.
Histories must be prefixes of actual positive-integer orbits, together with
congruence, valuation, inverse-tree, and Archimedean-height certificates.
The continuous \(2\)-adic extension is a useful shadow, not the native domain
of the conjecture.

### Native questions and answers

Questions include the next odd iterate, valuations of \(3n+1\), congruence
classes, stopping barriers, inverse branches, and candidate ranking
certificates. Answers must remain connected to positive integers.

### Possible birth mechanism

The valuation of \(3n+1\) creates the modulus or residue depth needed for the
next step. This is an unusually clean born-address phenomenon, but by itself
it is only Level A. A stronger event would construct a new descent certificate
type from a failed old certificate.

### Native cost and counterexample cascade

The profile must be at least two-dimensional:

\[
\operatorname{Cost}(h)
=
\bigl(\text{congruence or }2\text{-adic depth},
\text{Archimedean height}\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

An orbit that never reaches \(1\) could exhibit arbitrarily precise congruence
structure without genuine height descent. Any scalarization of (4) must be
justified, not chosen to manufacture monotonicity.

### Mandatory effectivity bridge

The generated structure must prove that every positive orbit reaches \(1\),
equivalently exclude divergent trajectories and cycles other than
\(1\to4\to2\to1\). A compact \(2\)-adic trajectory, average negative drift,
or verification through a finite bound is not enough.

### Controls and kill tests

- Test affine maps with known reaching-target, divergent, and nontrivial-cycle
  behavior.
- Separate forward orbit data from inverse-tree density.
- Require compatibility with actual integer height at every refinement.
- Kill any construction that proves only a statement in \(\mathbf Z_2\) or
  hides iteration time in an increasing modulus.
- Kill any proposed ranking function whose well-foundedness already assumes
  the conjecture.

## 5. Riemann-hypothesis realization requirements

### Native histories

The realization must first fix transform conventions and an exact admissible
Weil test class (including its reality, symmetry, regularity, and support or
decay hypotheses). Histories may then contain admissible test functions,
finite prime sums, explicit
formula evaluations, candidate spectral localizations, and exact error
bounds. A list of numerically verified zeros is not a native finite solution
of the global problem.

### Native questions and answers

Questions should live in an admissible analytic test class. They may ask for
positivity of a Weil-type form, localization of a spectral defect, or
compatibility of prime-side and zero-side data. Answers must retain exact
analytic control.

### Possible birth mechanism

A hypothetical off-critical defect could select a localized test function.
That is Level A unless the defect constructs a new admissible kernel or
positivity certificate with a universal role. Generated tests must remain in
the analytic class used by the explicit formula.

### Native cost and counterexample cascade

Costs include exponential type, bandwidth, smoothness, prime cutoff,
localization error, and spectral resolution. Under a separating and
continuation-complete explicit-formula game, an off-critical zero would have
to survive a cascade of increasingly localized admissible tests whose
prime-side errors remain controlled. That exhaustivity is not assumed here.

### Mandatory effectivity bridge

The method must derive the required positivity or an equivalent exact zero
constraint. A spectral bridge must construct a self-adjoint operator, identify
its spectrum with the nontrivial zeta zeros with the correct multiplicities
and completeness, and prove that this identification excludes off-critical
zeros. It cannot assume any of those properties as the missing theorem.

### Controls and kill tests

- Function-field zeta functions are the natural structural positive control.
- Finite Euler products and truncated explicit formulas test error accounting,
  not RH itself.
- Kill any generated test that leaves the admissible function class or whose
  prime-side cost becomes uncontrolled.
- Kill any spectral construction that inserts self-adjointness or positivity
  as an axiom.

## 6. AI realization requirements

### Native histories

An architecture theorem must first fix a task family, observer machine model,
quantifier order for universality and lower bounds, and cost semantics.
Histories are then interactions, tasks, compiler states, generated representations,
and proof-carrying residual programs. A hidden vector alone is only a shadow.

### Native questions and answers

Questions are executable probes or programs; answers include observations,
counterexamples, residual code, and certified failures of the current
representation. Born addresses are immediate; born probe types and formation
rules are the actual research target.

### Native cost and counterexample cascade

Cost includes local queries, compiled description length, runtime, retained
causal certificates, and size of the active question doctrine. A failure
cascade is a task sequence that continually forces genuinely new probe types.

### Mandatory effectivity bridge

The first serious architecture theorem should combine:

1. finite-demand external universality;
2. a strict lower bound against a declared bounded fixed-doctrine observer;
3. presentation invariance and causal stutter;
4. exact compositional reuse of birth certificates; and
5. a precise operational simulation class before any non-sofic claim.

### Controls and kill tests

- The born-address oracle is the Level-A baseline.
- Catalyst and higher-arity fixtures test whether linear features miss
  operational capability.
- Compare against adaptive querying, predicate invention, program synthesis,
  retrieval, and dynamic computation graphs after the object is frozen.
- Kill the architecture if it only activates entries from a hidden universal
  dictionary or if its compiler state trivially stores the full future tree.

## 7. Which proposed schemas remain typeable across all five frontiers?

### Genetic bisimulation: provisionally typable

Every frontier can in principle be organized around histories, locally
admissible actions, answers, and native continuations. A genetic bisimulation
could demand equivalence not only of answers and traces but of question-birth
events and their universal roles. No native instance has yet been constructed,
and the equivalence notion differs by subject.

### Causal compactness profile: schema only

Equation (2), or its order-valued replacement, is meaningful only after a
presentation-invariant native cost is supplied. Its values and growth classes
cannot be compared across frontiers without a theorem relating degree,
frequency, height, and exponential type. There is no such theorem here.

### Genesis curvature: conditionally typable

If two independent native continuations admit a comparison, one can ask
whether their resulting question languages agree. The defect is meaningful
only when the comparison is canonical and the difference has a native
realization. Otherwise curvature is presentation-dependent bookkeeping.

### Catalytic capability: not uniformly available

Ordered resource conversion is natural for computation and dissipative PDE.
It may illuminate positivity or effectivity elsewhere, but Hodge, Collatz,
and RH do not automatically carry one common monoidal resource order.

### Birth flux: rejected for now

No common additive quantity measuring new distinguishing capacity has been
defined or derived here. Degree, energy, height, and analytic bandwidth have
incompatible naive units and composition laws; that observation does not rule
out a future normalized, categorical, or order-valued invariant. “Birth flux”
remains dream language until derived in one native model.

## 8. A candidate non-headline native target

The first target should not be a weakened headline conjecture. A candidate
bridge is a **finite affine cellular-sheaf interrogation problem with
Dirichlet energy**. This is not yet an instantiated finite test or a minimality
claim. A complete fixture must specify, at minimum, a filled triangular
Čech nerve, oriented rational stalks and restriction maps, stalk inner
products and weights, and a prescribed degree-one datum \(b\). Its exact
question must use the triple-overlap differential \(d_1\): whether
\(d_1b=0\), and if so whether \([b]=0\in H^1\):

1. local sections live on cells of a finite complex;
2. questions expose restrictions, overlaps, or boundary ports;
3. answers can create the address of the next overlap or port;
4. a paired construction has identical declared unary and binary shadows but
   different triple-overlap extension behavior;
5. a resource component is deletion-sensitive while endpoint-invariant and is
   proved not to split as an inert factor;
6. the weighted degree-one Hodge Laplacian supplies exact positivity and
   energy; and
7. cocycle and exactness of the prescribed datum give literal obstruction and
   local-to-global success or failure.

Such a model cannot prove Hodge or Navier--Stokes. It is attractive because it
could force born addresses, gluing, positivity, higher-order compatibility,
and bounded assembly to coexist in one exact finite native realization. It
should be rejected if the zero section trivializes the task, compatible local
sections glue by definition, triple data are determined by binary restrictions,
the resource is an inert product factor, or all question births reduce to a
pre-enumerated adaptive order with no invariant beyond the fixed sheaf diagram.

## 9. Ranked research order

1. Freeze the causal-formation definitions and prove the externalization
   boundary: no absolute static nonrepresentability follows from history.
2. Execute independent finite controls for dependency, catalysis, higher
   arity, and branching; treat them as failures of separate forgetful maps.
3. Construct a coupled finite witness in which answer-dependent address,
   catalytic enabling, and higher-arity context interact without a direct
   product or inert factor.
4. Construct one finite example with a genuine Level-B born probe: prove it is
   not parameter selection from a fixed dictionary, give its
   presentation-invariant initial or minimal admission role, and separate it
   from a declared bounded old-observer class.
5. Define genetic bisimulation and produce two equal-fossil processes that it
   distinguishes with an operational experiment.
6. Build the finite cellular-sheaf/Dirichlet bridge and test stutter,
   confluence, presentation invariance, and strategic compactness.
7. Only then choose one native frontier control: linear Stokes and
   two-dimensional flow, Lefschetz \((1,1)\), positive-integer Collatz toy
   maps, or a function-field explicit-formula model.
8. Perform the collision audit against established adaptive, process,
   realizability, type-theoretic, resource, and renormalization frameworks.
9. Attempt a headline bridge only after a native control produces a theorem
   unavailable to its fixed-doctrine baseline.

The common ambition remains possible, but it has acquired a strict burden of
proof: the new foundation must generate native effectivity, not merely a new
language for describing old obstructions.
