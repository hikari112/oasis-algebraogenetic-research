# Counterfactual Constraint-Inversion Lab

## Status

**Protocol date:** 2026-08-10

**Abbreviation:** CCIL

**Evidence status:** experimental research protocol

The name is provisional. This document does not claim that CCIL is new, that
it is better than ordinary mathematical work, or that a counterfactual prompt
is evidence for a theorem. It makes the method falsifiable before the method
is used to support the project.

The companion [discovery-methods specification](./algebraogenesis-discovery-methods.md)
defines CCIL's place among Reflection, Meditation, Horizon View, Dream
Sequence, Wake, Frontier Sweep, Name/Collision Audit, and the Exact Finite Lab.
The [chronological retrospective](./algebraogenesis-dream-reflection-retrospective.md)
records the exploratory pilot that motivated this protocol.

## 1. Purpose

CCIL tests whether disciplined counterfactual construction produces more
verified mathematical progress than ordinary conjecture search.

Its central pipeline is

\[
\boxed{
\text{counterfactual extension}
\longrightarrow
\text{constraint inversion}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite compilation}
\longrightarrow
\text{elimination obstruction}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

A fictional theorem, future textbook, richer formal language, or hypothetical
device is useful only if its promised behavior can be decomposed into finite
typed operations. The fictional solution is then discarded. The primary
candidate is the smallest presentation-invariant obstruction to eliminating
the added capability from the original doctrine.

Let \(\mathcal L_0\) be the frozen original language or doctrine and let
\(\mathcal D_0\) be its typed derivation/object fragment, equipped with the
frozen semantic equivalence and cost model. A trial may adjoin a hypothetical
capability \(\chi\) with a behavioral specification:

\[
\mathcal L^+=\mathcal L_0[\chi\mid\operatorname{Spec}(\chi)].
\tag{2}
\]

Backward compilation seeks a partial elimination map

\[
\mathsf E:\mathcal D^+\dashrightarrow\mathcal D_0
\tag{3}
\]

on the generated derivation fragment \(\mathcal D^+\). The research object is
not \(\chi\) itself. It is the minimal invariant defect that prevents
\(\mathsf E\) from being total, conservative, and bounded-cost.

### Imagination as a coordinate map

CCIL does not model imagination as undirected noise. A conceptual premise can
act as a destination-shaped coordinate map: it says which roles should meet,
which distinctions should survive, and what the completed transition should
accomplish. Backward compilation can be more selective than unstructured
forward search because it asks only for prerequisites of that declared
behavior.

The map is not presumed correct. Philosophical resemblance does not establish
a continuous manifold, an isomorphism, a fixed point, or a necessary
resolution. CCIL earns mathematical content by finding the first place where
the imagined route fails to compile. That boundary may be a missing
definition, impossible naturality condition, cost lower bound,
nonrealizability theorem, or genuinely new construction target.

## 2. CCIL is not the Dream Sequence

| feature | Dream Sequence | CCIL |
|---|---|---|
| primary move | divergent association | invert one declared constraint |
| allowed invention | unrestricted and mutually inconsistent | one specified hypothetical capability |
| main output | motifs, finite shadows, contradictions | compiled dependency graph and elimination obstruction |
| comparison | different dreams need not share a language | all arms receive the same frozen packet |
| success | a useful survivor or better question | a verified construction, no-go, or proof-obligation reduction |
| compulsory downstream phase | Wake | backward compilation, Wake, and blind evaluation |

DS can suggest a capability worth testing in CCIL. CCIL cannot retroactively
turn a dream statement into evidence.

## 3. Experimental arms

Every arm receives the same frozen problem packet and the same downstream
verification.

1. **Future textbook.** Reconstruct the definitions and lemmas that a later
   textbook would need if the target were routine.
2. **Fictional capability.** Specify a device or richer language that performs
   the desired task, then remove every oracle-like operation.
3. **Unrestricted association.** Generate analogies and candidate structures
   without a prescribed route.
4. **Ordinary conjecture search.** Develop the weakest plausible lemmas
   directly from the current definitions.
5. **Disciplined constraint inversion.** Assume the declared extension exists,
   infer its necessary internal structure backward, and compile that structure
   before making any claim.

The first four are comparison arms. The fifth is the method under test. No arm
is privileged in scoring.

## 4. Separated roles

Roles should be separated whenever practical.

- The **registrar** freezes the protocol, prompts, seeds, budgets, controls,
  scoring rules, and stop conditions.
- The **packet curator** supplies exact known results, open obligations,
  admissible primitives, and forbidden assumptions.
- The **generator** produces candidates under one assigned arm without live
  research.
- The **constraint inverter** converts promised behavior into necessary
  observables, state, operations, transports, and costs.
- The **compiler** replaces prose with typed finite objects, maps, equations,
  quantifiers, and replayable certificates.
- The **adversary** constructs trivial, quotient, symmetry, gauge, aggregation,
  random-access, and presentation countermodels.
- The **verifier** audits proof and executable evidence without knowing the
  generation arm.
- The **evaluator** scores canonicalized result cards after semantic
  deduplication.
- The **literature classifier** runs a Bright Data Frontier Sweep and
  Name/Collision Audit only after the mathematical artifact is frozen.
- The **archivist** retains accepted, rejected, collided, and unresolved paths
  with provenance.

A generator never verifies its own candidate. Arm identity remains sealed
until evaluation is complete.

## 5. Phase gates

### Phase 0 - preregistration

Freeze:

- the research question and proof-obligation graph;
- definitions and already established results;
- positive, negative, and placebo controls;
- prompts for every arm;
- model version, sampling settings, seeds, time, and token limits;
- maximum candidates per trial;
- scoring rules and stop conditions; and
- the source-access policy.

**Gate G0:** The packet and protocol receive immutable digests. Any later
change creates a new experiment.

### Phase 1 - counterfactual extension

Each generator returns at most three extension cards describing:

- the behavior newly made possible;
- inputs and outputs;
- the distinction the current theory cannot express or preserve;
- local operations the extension appears to require; and
- its strongest anticipated counterexample.

No historical names, citations, web searches, or novelty claims are allowed.

**Gate G1:** Reject cards that offer only a metaphor, desired conclusion, or
undefined capability.

### Phase 2 - constraint inversion and de-magic

Work backward from each promised output:

1. What information must be available when the output is produced?
2. Which distinctions must remain materialized until then?
3. Which operation transports or exposes each distinction?
4. Which capability is secretly an oracle, random-access address, equality
   solver, unbounded compiler, or hidden target witness?
5. What is the weakest finite structure that could implement the remainder?

Every inferred requirement is classified as:

- supplied input;
- derived invariant;
- persistent state;
- local operation;
- external oracle;
- cost-bearing compilation; or
- unresolved assumption.

**Gate G2:** Every output must trace to declared finite operations. Cards
retaining an unimplemented oracle are rejected or quarantined.

### Phase 3 - finite compilation

Compile each surviving extension into a theorem card containing:

- typed carrier objects;
- state, process, effect, and probe roles;
- maps and transition laws;
- finite marks or ports;
- quantifier order;
- normalization and cost ledger;
- an exact target statement;
- proof dependencies;
- executable or symbolic witness format; and
- an explicit claim boundary.

The compiler must distinguish endpoint state, transported process, persistent
commitment, unrealized alternative, and future query whenever their types
differ.

**Gate G3:** No undefined maps, hidden global state, free semantic addresses,
or uncharged batch operations remain.

### Phase 4 - obstruction

Run all preregistered controls before inventing candidate-specific tests:

- trivial object or trivial action;
- kernel-collapsed finite quotient;
- matched conventional implementation;
- relabeling, coordinate, and gauge invariance;
- reversal and stutter;
- presentation or basis change;
- delete-the-proposed-structure ablation;
- average-before-square versus square-before-average;
- malformed certificate and replay mismatch;
- bounded-cost and random-access controls; and
- hidden-circularity canary.

The adversary then supplies the strongest custom falsifier.

**Gate G4:** A card advances only if it survives, or if its failure yields an
exact independently reproducible no-go result. Attractive failed prose does
not count.

### Phase 5 - verification

The verifier assigns one status:

- **proved**;
- **finite theorem verified with a stated horizon**;
- **conditional on a named lemma**;
- **counterexample verified**;
- **unresolved**; or
- **false**.

Executable evidence must test the stated object rather than a weaker proxy.
Finite enumeration never silently becomes a universal theorem.

**Gate G5:** Only proved results and exact counterexamples receive primary
discovery credit.

### Phase 6 - blind evaluation

Strip arm-specific language and canonicalize every result into one schema.
Cluster semantic duplicates before scoring.

A result is one **verified gap-reduction unit** only if it:

1. was absent from the frozen packet;
2. is proved or independently verified;
3. survives the preregistered controls;
4. strictly closes or narrows a named proof obligation;
5. adds no assumption equivalent to the desired conclusion; and
6. remains distinct after semantic deduplication.

Exact no-go theorems count when they eliminate a previously live route.

**Gate G6:** Freeze the canonical result card—statement, quantifiers,
equivalences, cost model, dependencies, evidence horizon, and claim boundary—
with an immutable digest. Literature search cannot modify this card. A revision
returns to compilation and receives a new digest.

### Phase 7 - Frontier Sweep and Name/Collision Audit

Only now search the literature. The sweep is broad in field but narrow in
structural role; the collision audit compares frozen definitions and
quantifiers rather than prose similarity.

Classify each verified unit as:

- direct prior art;
- a known theorem in new notation;
- known components with project-specific synthesis;
- an existing unsolved conjecture;
- an apparently unclassified candidate; or
- unresolved because search coverage is incomplete.

Bright Data search outputs must retain dated query, source, and failed-search
ledgers. Absence from a finite search is never evidence of novelty.

**Gate G7:** Novelty language remains provisional until independent expert
review.

### Phase 8 - Oasis admission

A verified unit enters Oasis only if it changes the formal proof-obligation
graph.

- A surviving construction adds a typed generator, transport, observer, or
  compiler.
- A no-go adds a forbidden collapse or mandatory mark.
- A collision becomes a matched standard baseline.
- An unresolved card remains quarantined.

Rejected and admitted paths are both retained. The completed algebra is a
fossil of this admission history, not a substitute for it.

## 7. Preregistered comparison

The first confirmatory comparison should use:

- eight frozen Oasis proof gaps;
- five arms;
- four independent seeds per arm;
- at most three theorem cards per trial; and
- identical context, configuration, token, and time budgets.

This gives \(8\times5\times4=160\) trials. Generation uses a fresh context for
every trial and no web, literature, historical-name, or cross-arm access.
Verification and scoring occur only after semantic deduplication.

### Primary metric

\[
\operatorname{verified\ yield}
=
\frac{\text{verified gap-reduction units}}
     {\text{generation tokens}/10{,}000}.
\tag{4}
\]

Secondary metrics include:

- verification minutes per verified unit;
- false-theorem rate;
- circularity-canary failure rate;
- placebo rejection rate;
- semantic collision rate;
- proportion yielding exact no-go results;
- proportion requiring undeclared oracles;
- proof-obligation reduction;
- provisional novelty rate after classification; and
- survival under rephrasing and presentation controls.

Compare arms in paired problem-and-seed blocks using a preregistered
randomization test. Report effect sizes and uncertainty. Do not select only the
most vivid examples.

### Frozen controls

The initial suite includes:

1. **positive calibration:** marked Fox curvature equals relator Hamming defect;
2. **negative calibration:** a global Hodge spectral gap implies freeness;
3. **shuffled placebo:** interfaces are reassigned across incompatible native
   targets; and
4. **hidden circularity canary:** the target witness is encoded indirectly in
   an admissibility predicate or decoder domain.

The positive calibration must be reconstructed without access to its known
answer. The negative and circularity controls must be rejected.

## 8. Contamination barriers

- No literature or web access during generation or compilation.
- No cross-arm conversation or access to earlier candidates.
- One fresh context per trial.
- Arm labels hidden from compilers, adversaries, verifiers, and evaluators.
- Identical background packet and budgets across arms.
- Historical names and fabricated citations prohibited.
- Literature results cannot rewrite the frozen candidate.
- Prompt, source, and transcript digests retained.
- A model or configuration change restarts the affected block.
- Tiny pilots are labeled exploratory and excluded from confirmatory
  statistics.

These barriers do not pretend that language models are statistically
independent. They prevent the most direct information leaks and keep the
comparison interpretable.

## 9. Stop rules

### Candidate stop

Reject or quarantine immediately when:

- an assumption restates the target;
- an oracle remains unimplemented;
- the object is ill-typed;
- replay disagrees with the claimed transition;
- a preregistered falsifier succeeds;
- the positive control fails;
- the result produces no strict gap reduction; or
- evidence is only metaphor, analogy, or finite numerics supporting an
  unrestricted claim.

### Trial stop

Each trial ends at the fixed budget or after three cards. No rescue prompts or
arm-specific extensions are permitted.

### Experiment stop

Run the preregistered sample completely. Do not stop because one arm looks
successful. Pause only for contamination, configuration drift, corrupted
artifacts, or failed blinding; restart the affected block.

### Promotion stop

No result is promoted without two independent audits, or one proof audit plus
reproducible executable verification. Disagreement leaves the result
unresolved.

## 10. Exploratory pilot A - localization before quadratic energy

This pilot is a retrospective calibration, not part of the 160-trial
comparison. Its established finite input is documented in the
[conjecture ladder and Fox--Hodge bridge](./endogenous-conjecture-ladder-and-fox-hodge-bridge.md)
and its [executable certificate](./genesis-marked-fox-hodge-bridge.mjs).

For a permutation \(P\) of a finite set \(V\), aggregation before a nonlinear
operation erases the entire boundary:

\[
\sum_{v\in V}(\delta_{Pv}-\delta_v)=0.
\tag{5}
\]

Keeping atomic fibers until after squaring instead gives

\[
\frac{1}{2|V|}
\sum_{v\in V}
\left\|\delta_{Pv}-\delta_v\right\|^2
=d_{\mathrm{Ham}}(P,I).
\tag{6}
\]

The backwards question, “what must a future Fox--Hodge theorem preserve in
order to see relation error?”, therefore recovered a real load-bearing rule:

\[
\boxed{\text{localize before applying quadratic energy}.}
\tag{7}
\]

The matched placebo, “a global Hodge spectral gap implies freeness,” fails on
finite quotient actions. The pilot produced one verified interface rule and
one counterexample, not a major-problem result.

## 11. Exploratory pilot B - conclusion-free native-germ reflection

The backward-compiler role assumed that the architectural ladder and all four
native realizations eventually succeeded, then removed any conclusion-specific
content. It produced the following generated theorem card.

For each input \(x\), posit:

- a native candidate type \(\mathcal N_x\);
- a native defect \(D_x:\mathcal N_x\to[0,\infty]\), whose zero set is the
  native solution space;
- a directed finite-challenge category \(\mathcal Q_x\);
- finite or compact response objects
  \(A_x:\mathcal Q_x^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Comp}\);
- a completion
  \[
  \widehat A_x=\varprojlim_{q\in\mathcal Q_x}A_x(q);
  \]
- a conclusion-blind admissible-germ space
  \(\mathcal G_x\subseteq\widehat A_x\);
- local defects \(\delta_{x,q}\), with
  \[
  \Delta_x(g)=\sup_q\delta_{x,q}(g_q);
  \]
- and a native observation nerve
  \(\nu_x:\mathcal N_x\to\widehat A_x\).

The missing bridge would be a total realization map

\[
\mathsf{Real}_x:\mathcal G_x\longrightarrow\mathcal N_x
\tag{8}
\]

and a nondecreasing modulus \(\omega_x\), with \(\omega_x(0)=0\), such that
for **every** admissible germ, including bad germs,

\[
\nu_x\!\left(\mathsf{Real}_x(g)\right)=g,
\qquad
D_x\!\left(\mathsf{Real}_x(g)\right)
\leq
\omega_x\!\left(\Delta_x(g)\right).
\tag{9}
\]

The card is deliberately conclusion-free. It does not assume that any
zero-defect germ exists. It forbids:

- defining \(\mathcal G_x\) as the observations of already known solutions;
- defining \(\mathsf{Real}_x\) only on good germs;
- choosing compactness or coercivity from a known solution;
- letting local defects query the truth of the theorem; and
- returning a formal limit when the native target requires a finite cycle,
  well-founded rank, coercive rule, or closed operator.

### Decisive finite toy

For \(b\in\mathbf F_2\), let

\[
\mathcal N_b=\mathbf F_2^3
\]

and consider

\[
x+y=0,
\qquad
y+z=0,
\qquad
x+z=b.
\tag{10}
\]

Instantiate every interface in the theorem card as follows.

- \(\mathcal Q_b\) is the directed inclusion poset of all nonempty subsets of
  \(\{x,y,z\}\).
- \(A_b(S)=\mathbf F_2^S\), with ordinary restriction maps for inclusions.
- \(\widehat A_b\) is the limit of this restriction diagram. It is canonically
  the set of compatible families of partial assignments, hence canonically
  \(\mathbf F_2^3\).
- Admissibility is fixed **independently of the conclusion** by
  \[
  \mathcal G_b=\widehat A_b.
  \]
  Thus no compatible germ may be removed because it violates an equation.
- \(\nu_b:\mathcal N_b\to\widehat A_b\) sends a triple to all of its
  restrictions. It is a bijection.
- \(\mathsf{Real}_b=\nu_b^{-1}\) glues the compatible family. It is the same
  map for \(b=0\) and \(b=1\).
- The only nonzero local defects are
  \[
  \delta_{xy}=[x+y\ne0],\qquad
  \delta_{yz}=[y+z\ne0],\qquad
  \delta^b_{xz}=[x+z\ne b],
  \]
  and \(\Delta_b\) is their maximum.
- The native defect is
  \[
  D_b(x,y,z)=
  \max\{[x+y\ne0],[y+z\ne0],[x+z\ne b]\},
  \]
  and \(\omega_b(t)=t\).

Consequently, for every germ \(g\in\mathcal G_b\),

\[
\nu_b\mathsf{Real}_b(g)=g,
\qquad
D_b(\mathsf{Real}_b(g))=\Delta_b(g).
\tag{11}
\]

Exhaustive enumeration gives exactly two zero-defect assignments for \(b=0\),
namely `000` and `111`, and none for \(b=1\), although deleting any one of the
three equations makes the \(b=1\) system satisfiable. The all-zero restriction
family belongs to \(\mathcal G_1\) by the explicit admissibility definition, so
the total realizer must return `000` and equation (11) reports defect one. It
cannot hide unsatisfiability by shrinking its domain.

This elementary test kills three common cheats: mistaking independent local
solutions for a compatible germ, hiding solvability in the realizer's domain,
and placing a truth bit in the decoder. Equation (9) remains a generated common
lemma, not an established theorem for any major frontier. Equation (11) is only
its exact finite calibration.

## 12. Exploratory pilot C - finite refusal and return packets

The future-textbook arm imagined a finite situation \(s\), a finite demand set
\(D(s)\), replies \(R(s,d)\), and updates \(s[d/r]\). Persistent refusal is
the greatest fixed point

\[
Z=
\left\{
s:\quad
\forall d\in D(s)\quad
\exists r\in R(s,d),\quad
s[d/r]\in Z
\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]

Counterfactual bisimulation then minimizes states while preserving the entire
demand/reply policy, including replies that were not selected on the realized
path. A charged cycle in this quotient was called a **finite return packet**:
a finite exact law identifying a later demand/reply structure with a rescaled
earlier one while retaining all unrealized replies.

This may be a useful obstruction language for counterfactual process memory.
It is not presently justified that Hodge, Navier--Stokes, Collatz, RH, or AI
failure has a finite return packet; the assertion is false for general
computable systems. The object remains `CCIL_PROPOSAL` until a native bounded
fixture and noncollapse theorem exist.

## 13. Relation to algebraogenesis

CCIL operationalizes one possible algebraogenetic move:

1. a counterfactual extension proposes a future distinction;
2. constraint inversion identifies present structure required to support it;
3. finite compilation builds the weakest typed observable or transport system
   that could carry it;
4. obstruction determines whether the structure is necessary, redundant,
   conventional, inconsistent, or genuinely gap-reducing; and
5. admission changes the proof-obligation graph only after verification.

This is stronger than accumulating ideas and weaker than a theory of
mathematical discovery. It retains the path because exact failed eliminations
can become no-go theorems.

## 14. Explicit nonclaims

This protocol does not claim:

- that counterfactual prompting proves theorems;
- that imaginative language is evidence of novelty;
- that future-textbook or fictional-device prompts describe future
  mathematics;
- that one model's samples are statistically independent;
- that the exploratory pilot compares methods reliably;
- that finite verification proves an unrestricted theorem;
- that Hodge terminology supplies geometric Hodge theory;
- that renamed Hamming tests bypass the definition of soficity;
- that a prior-art search can establish priority;
- that the method measures creativity in general;
- that algebraogenesis is already a new foundational field;
- that the protocol bears on the four major problems without separate native
  reflection theorems; or
- that the process replaces peer review, proof assistants, or expert
  mathematical judgment.
