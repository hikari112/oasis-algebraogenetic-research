# Genesis Higher Question Quotients: relation policies and coherent-lift obstruction

## Status: exact classification inside one-filler-generated 2-group semantics

The free higher attachment answered the unrestricted question

\[
\mathscr F_T(N)=\{n\in N:\partial n=T\}
\]

by the universal crossed module

\[
M=\mathbb Z[Q],
\qquad
Q=P/\langle T\rangle.
\]

This note asks what is actually forced when that unrestricted question is not
assumed.  The answer is not a binary choice between the infinite free object
and the thin zero-`pi_2` object.  There is an exact lattice of relation
policies.  Finite quotients can retain nonzero Postnikov classes, and the
smallest such quotient has only four elements.

The central result is a question canonically derived after a relation policy
has been selected.  For any such policy, the Postnikov class is nonzero exactly
when the original central-extension cocycle has no globally coherent lift into
that filler algebra.  Thus the next obstruction is not an independently named
associator.  It is an empty coherent-lift type computed from the preceding
extension and the selected coefficient sequence.

The executable companion is
[`genesis-higher-question-quotients.mjs`](genesis-higher-question-quotients.mjs).

## 1. A variance-aware universal-repair pattern

The Level-1 homology cover represents a contravariant trivialization
presheaf:

\[
\operatorname{Triv}_{\alpha}(Y)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Graph}/G}(Y,E_G).
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, the category of graph objects equipped with a trivialization
has a terminal object.

The Level-2 filler question has the opposite polarity:

\[
\mathscr F_T(N)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{XMod}_P}(F_P(T),N).
\tag{2}
\]

Its category of pointed fillers has an initial object.  Both levels instantiate
one dimension-neutral pattern:

> Admit a typed solution functor and attach its universal solution object.

But (1) does not imply (2).  A terminal object in a graph slice cannot select
an initial object in an independently chosen category of higher cells.  The
existing Level-1 locality doctrine therefore does not force crossed-module
semantics.

## 2. What is forced after higher typing

Fix the exact failed-naturality data

\[
P=\langle W,R\rangle\cong D_8,
\qquad
T=[W,R]=(WR)^2,
\tag{3}
\]

and let

\[
U_P:\mathbf{XMod}_P\longrightarrow\mathbf{Set}/P
\tag{4}
\]

forget a crossed `P`-module down to its boundary map.  Let

\[
\tau_T:1\to P
\]

select `T`.

### Theorem 1: universal-arrow characterization

The following are equivalent:

1. the full boundary-fiber functor `F_T` is represented;
2. the comma category `(tau_T downarrow U_P)` has an initial object; and
3. `tau_T` admits a universal arrow to `U_P`.

When these conditions hold, the representing pair is unique up to unique
isomorphism preserving its universal filler.

For the central involution `T` in (3), that initial object is

\[
F_P(T)=\mathbb Z[Q],
\qquad
Q=P/\langle T\rangle\cong C_2^2,
\tag{5}
\]

with regular `Q`-action and parity-augmentation boundary.

This separates three choices that must not be conflated:

- promoting the raw route comparison to invertible higher semantics;
- admitting the entire boundary fiber as the meaningful question; and
- constructing the representer.

The first two are semantic typing decisions.  Once they are made, the third
is forced by the universal property.

If the connected one-object route semantics is required to have invertible
1- and 2-cells, with strict vertical and horizontal composition and
interchange, crossed modules are the standard algebraic normal form: their
category is equivalent to the category of strict 2-groups.  This makes crossed
modules canonical inside that strict one-object doctrine, but it does not prove
that genesis must reject noninvertible, many-object, or nongroupoidal higher
cells.

## 3. Every generated relation policy is a quotient

Let

\[
A=\ker(\epsilon\bmod2)\subseteq M.
\tag{6}
\]

Consider a pointed crossed `P`-module `(B,b)` satisfying

\[
\partial_B b=T
\]

and generated by the complete `P`-orbit of `b`.  The universal map is

\[
\Phi_b:M\to B,
\qquad
[q]\longmapsto q\cdot b.
\tag{7}
\]

### Theorem 2: relation-policy classification

Every such pointed object has a canonical pointed isomorphism

\[
\boxed{
(M/L,[1]+L)\cong(B,b)
}
\tag{8}
\]

for the uniquely determined `Q`-stable relation subgroup

\[
L=\ker\Phi_b\subseteq A.
\tag{9}
\]

Conversely, every `Q`-stable subgroup (9) gives a valid one-filler-generated
crossed `P`-module.

### Proof

The map (7) is the unique filler-preserving map from the free object.  Peiffer
gives `T dot b=b`, so `q dot b` is independent of the chosen representative of
`q in Q`.  Orbit generation makes the map surjective.  Its kernel is the
canonical relation subgroup and is `Q`-stable.  Boundary
compatibility implies that every kernel element has even augmentation, hence
`L=ker Phi_b` lies in `A`.  Conversely, a `Q`-stable `L` inside `A` preserves
the parity boundary and induces the quotient `Q`-action, so `M/L` is a crossed
`P`-module with distinguished filler `[1]+L`.  \(\square\)

The quotient represents a restricted filler question.  If

\[
\ell=\sum_q n_q[q]\in L,
\]

then a target filler `n` obeys the relation

\[
\ell(n)=\sum_q n_q(q\cdot n)=0.
\tag{10}
\]

The element `T` fixes every filler of boundary `T`, so `q dot n` is independent
of the chosen lift from `Q` to `P`; Peiffer also makes all these translates
commute.  Thus (10) is well-defined, even when the ambient target crossed
module is nonabelian.  There is a natural bijection

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{XMod}_P}(M/L,N)
\cong
\{n\in\partial_N^{-1}(T):\ell(n)=0\text{ for every }\ell\in L\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus a relation policy is literally a policy about which fillers count as
answers.

Important cases are:

| Relation subgroup | Domain | Represented filler question |
|---|---|---|
| `L=0` | `Z[Q]` | every filler of boundary `T` |
| `L=I_Q=ker epsilon` | `Z` | `P`-fixed fillers, unrestricted order |
| `L=ker(epsilon mod 4)` | `C_4` | `P`-fixed fillers of order dividing four |
| `L=A` | `C_2` | `P`-fixed involutive fillers |

The final row is the thin inclusion.  It is only one endpoint of the relation
lattice.

## 4. Exact survival criterion for the higher class

Let

\[
B_L=M/L,
\qquad
K_L=A/L.
\]

There is an exact coefficient sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow K_L
\longrightarrow B_L
\xrightarrow{\rho_L}\mathbb F_2
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]

Fix a normalized cocycle

\[
c\in Z^2(Q;\mathbb F_2)
\tag{13}
\]

representing the class `[c]` of the nonsplit central extension

\[
1\to\langle T\rangle\to P\to Q\to1.
\]

### Theorem 3: quotient and cocycle-lift criterion

The Postnikov class of `B_L -> P` is

\[
\boxed{
k_L=\beta_L([c])
\in H^3(Q;K_L),
}
\tag{14}
\]

where `beta_L` is the connecting map of (12).  Equivalently, it is the
coefficient image of the universal class.

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
k_L=0
\iff
[c]\in\operatorname{im}
\bigl(H^2(Q;B_L)\to H^2(Q;\mathbb F_2)\bigr).
}
\tag{15}
\]

Thus the class survives exactly when the central-extension cocycle has no
globally coherent `B_L`-valued cocycle lift.

The unrestricted object `L=0` retains the canonical `A`-valued class.  The
thin object `L=A` has `K_L=0` and collapses it.  Between them, either outcome
is possible.

## 5. The policy-derived next question

Define the coherent-lift type

\[
\operatorname{CocLift}_{c}(B_L)
=
\{F\in Z^2(Q;B_L):\rho_LF=c\}.
\tag{16}
\]

If `c'=c+delta a` for a normalized 1-cochain `a`, choose a normalized lift
`a_tilde in C^1(Q;B_L)`.  Then

\[
F\longmapsto F+\delta\widetilde a
\]

is a bijection from `CocLift_c(B_L)` to `CocLift_{c'}(B_L)`.  The bijection
depends on the chosen lift, but emptiness does not.

Theorem 3 becomes

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{CocLift}_{c}(B_L)=\varnothing
\iff
k_L\ne0.
}
\tag{17}
\]

This is the next question canonically derived from the selected relation policy
and coefficient sequence.  Every value of `c` has a pointwise lift because
`rho_L` is surjective.  The obstruction is the failure of those local choices
to satisfy the global 2-cocycle law.  The raw `W,R,T` data do not by themselves
select that policy or demand promotion to dimension three.

For any normalized cochain lift `F_0`,

\[
\kappa_L=\delta F_0,
\qquad
\delta\kappa_L=0.
\tag{18}
\]

Changing `F_0` changes `kappa_L` by a `K_L`-valued coboundary.  Equation (17),
not an independently declared `H^3` table, is the exact candidate Level-3
obstruction question visible after the Level-2 relation policy is fixed.

## 6. One-axis descent is only a detector

Let

\[
x=WR\langle T\rangle\in Q,
\qquad
\langle x\rangle\cong C_2.
\tag{19}
\]

For the chosen section cocycle, `mu(x,x)=1`; equivalently, the preimage of
`x` contains the generated order-four square root `WR`.

For the distinguished odd filler `b=[1]+L`, where
`rho_L(b)=1`, define

\[
m_B=x\cdot b-b\in K_L.
\tag{20}
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{res}_{\langle x\rangle}k_L
=
[m_B]
\in
\frac{\ker(1+x)}{(x-1)K_L}.
\tag{21}
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{res}_{\langle x\rangle}k_L\ne0
\iff
m_B\notin(x-1)K_L.
\tag{22}
\]

This has a direct filler interpretation:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{res}_{\langle x\rangle}k_L\ne0
\iff
\partial_B^{-1}(T)
\text{ contains no }x\text{-fixed filler}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

Indeed, `m_B=(x-1)a` for `a in K_L` exactly when `b-a` is an odd
`x`-fixed filler.

For a general nonidentity `t in Q`, the restriction formula includes the
extension residue:

\[
\operatorname{res}_{\langle t\rangle}k_L
=
\mu(t,t)[t\cdot b-b].
\]

Thus the fixed-odd-filler test detects the transport residue.  It is equivalent
to nonvanishing of the actual Postnikov restriction only when `mu(t,t)=1`; if
`mu(t,t)=0`, that subgroup restriction vanishes regardless of transport.

Equation (23) is only a criterion for the cyclic restriction.  It is a
sufficient detector of global nonvanishing, not a global equivalence.  Mixed
transport can retain the full class even when this one-axis residue vanishes.

## 7. The minimum finite obstruction-faithful carrier cardinality

If `B` is finite and the induced boundary

\[
\rho:B\longrightarrow\langle T\rangle\cong C_2
\]

is surjective, then

\[
|B|=2|\ker\rho|.
\]

A nonzero Postnikov class requires a nonzero coefficient kernel, so

\[
|B|\ge4.
\tag{24}
\]

The bound is sharp in several inequivalent ways.

### Swapped binary fillers

Take

\[
B=C_2^2,
\qquad
\rho(u,v)=u+v,
\qquad
b=(1,0),
\tag{25}
\]

define `partial_B(z)=T^{rho(z)}`, let `x` swap the coordinates, and let a
complementary generator `y` act trivially.  The odd fillers `(1,0)` and
`(0,1)` are exchanged, while

\[
K_B=\langle(1,1)\rangle.
\]

The cyclic residue is `(1,1)` and is nonzero.

### Oriented order-four fillers

Take

\[
B=C_4,
\qquad
\rho(n)=n\bmod2,
\qquad
b=1,
\tag{26}
\]

define `partial_B(n)=T^{rho(n)}`, let `x` act by inversion, and let a
complementary generator `y` act trivially.  Then `x b-b=2`, the nonzero kernel
element, so the cyclic and global classes survive.

### Fixed order-four fillers: essential global survival

Use the same `C_4` boundary with trivial action by both generators of `Q`.  The
cyclic residue now
vanishes because every filler is `x`-fixed.  Nevertheless the global class is
nonzero.

Writing `u(a,b)=a` and `v(a,b)=b`, so that

\[
H^*(C_2^2;C_2)=C_2[u,v]
\]

and the `D_8` extension class as

\[
[c]=u^2+uv,
\]

the connecting map for

\[
0\to C_2\to C_4\to C_2\to0
\]

gives

\[
\beta[c]=u^2v+uv^2\ne0.
\tag{27}
\]

Indeed, the coefficient Bockstein has `beta(u)=u^2` and `beta(v)=v^2` and
obeys the derivation rule, so
`beta(u^2+uv)=u^2v+uv^2`, a nonzero polynomial in `F_2[u,v]`.

In fact

\[
u^2v+uv^2=uv(u+v).
\]

The three proper nontrivial subgroups of `C_2^2` are the lines on which
`u=0`, `v=0`, and `u+v=0`.  The class restricts to zero on every one of them.
It is therefore an essential cohomology class: globally nonzero but invisible
on every proper subgroup.

This is a decisive falsifier: infinity is required for the unrestricted
representer, not for a nonzero higher class, and nonvanishing on any proper
subgroup is not necessary for global nonvanishing.

The executable exhausts finite abelian `B` of cardinalities two and four, all
surjective boundaries, all commuting involutive `Q`-actions preserving the
boundary, and all orbit-generating odd pointed fillers.  It independently
solves both sides of (15), audits all three order-two subgroup restrictions,
and finds exactly eight of the 27 pointings globally nonzero but invisible on
every proper subgroup.  It also verifies the minimum (24).  Isomorphic
pointings may occur separately in the raw census; the claim does not extend to
nonabelian targets.

## 8. Semantic quotient and model invariance

The relation-policy theorem classifies orbit-generated abelian quotients of
the free crossed module.  Other presentations retain the same obstruction
only under an appropriate homotopy condition.

### Theorem 4: 2-type invariance

A weak equivalence of connected 2-types that compatibly identifies `pi_1`,
`pi_2`, and their action carries the Postnikov class to the corresponding
class.  Therefore the nerve, corresponding reduced simplicial 2-type, and
crossed complex of the same free crossed module retain the class.  A coherent
2-group presentation retains it when it is biequivalent to that 2-group.  A
topological presentation retains it when a 2-cell is freely adjoined along
`T` to `K(P,1)` and the associated 2-type is taken.  An arbitrary presentation
complex or arbitrary reduced simplicial 2-type need not have the same
`pi_2`, action, or Postnikov class.

The class can collapse when a semantic operation:

- 1-truncates the object;
- makes `T=1` strictly;
- makes all admitted fillers locally unique;
- imposes a relation subgroup `L` satisfying the lifting condition in (15);
  or
- attaches a higher cell whose boundary kills the relevant `pi_2` class.

Ordinary action groupoids and `BP` are only 1-types; they retain `T` as
1-transport but have no comparable `pi_2` carrier.  Locally thin
2-groupoidal cores have `pi_2=0`.  General posetal or bicategorical nerves do
not collapse for that reason alone.  Groupoid-valued spans or profunctors are
underdetermined: their invertible generated core must first be shown
biequivalent to the desired 2-type.

## 9. A cohomological Stokes direction

The new tower is

\[
\rho_L(F_0)=c,
\qquad
\kappa_L=\delta F_0,
\qquad
\delta\kappa_L=0.
\tag{28}
\]

The datum has local coefficient lifts but no globally closed lift precisely
when `k_L` is nonzero.  This is a genuine local-to-global obstruction and a
connecting-homomorphism construction.  It is closer to a native Stokes
mechanism than installing an unrelated Laplacian.

It is still not Hodge theory.  No metric, adjoint, harmonic representative,
polarization, or algebraic-cycle comparison has been generated.  The next
geometric target is to derive a transport-natural pairing or energy on the
coherent-lift complex and determine whether relation policies have a canonical
minimum-residual representative.

## 10. Honest boundary and next gate

This note proves, inside one-filler-generated abelian crossed-module
semantics:

- the exact relation-policy classification `M/L`;
- the `L`-restricted filler subfunctor represented by `M/L`;
- the coefficient-pushforward formula for the Postnikov class;
- the global coherent-cocycle-lift survival criterion;
- the cyclic fixed-filler detector and its strict limitation;
- the all-proper-subgroup essentiality of the trivial-action `C_4` control;
- cardinality four as the minimum finite nonzero carrier; and
- invariance under equivalent presentations of the same 2-type.

It does not prove:

- that Level-1 graph locality forces higher typing;
- that all meaningful higher fillers must be invertible, strict, abelian, or
  generated by one orbit;
- that nonvanishing selects the unrestricted doctrine;
- that the `C_4` and `C_2^2` carriers are canonically preferred;
- a generated 3-cell representing the empty coherent-lift type;
- non-soficity, noncomputability, or an infinite-depth theorem;
- Hodge theory, Stokes' theorem, or a consequence for an open conjecture; or
- a new AI architecture.

The next theorem target is now exact:

> Promote the empty coherent-lift type (17) into dimension three, classify the
> relation policies of its universal representer, and determine whether the
> resulting next obstruction is again the connecting image of data generated
> at the preceding level.

That is the first plausible recursive law for algebraogenesis:

\[
\text{current extension class}
\longrightarrow
\text{empty coherent-lift question}
\longrightarrow
\text{universal higher representer}
\longrightarrow
\text{next connecting obstruction}.
\tag{29}
\]
