# Endogenous admission under composite collapse closure

## Status and claim boundary

This is a theorem-program and adversarial classification note. It does not
claim a new field, a new prior-art result, non-soficity, an AI complexity
separation, or progress on Hodge, Navier--Stokes, or any other major open
problem. It contains no executable-evidence claim and no literature naming
pass.

The exact mathematical contribution recorded here is narrower:

1. six common ways in which an alleged history-derived universal repair can
   collapse into a construction already available to the old doctrine;
2. a single composite closure against which future family theorems must be
   quantified;
3. an elementary classification of the active-subspace sign-trivializing
   cover, including its exact finite-state interpretation and its limits; and
4. one provisional next construction based on guarded final coalgebras,
   together with the warning that it may merely expose a seventh standard
   completion route.

The definitions of Level A, Level B, Level C, universal birth, genetic
bisimulation, semantic stutter, presentation invariance, complexity, and
finite externalization are inherited from
[Endogenous expressibility foundations](./endogenous-expressibility-foundations.md).
The distinctions between a born probe and a born former are calibrated in
[Frontier realizations](./endogenous-expressibility-frontier-realizations.md).
The finite coequalizer example and its Karoubi collapse are recorded in
[Coupled calibration](./endogenous-expressibility-coupled-calibration.md).

## 1. Why absolute escape is ill-posed

The word *new* must always be typed relative to a previously frozen doctrine.
Without that discipline, each of the strongest proposed exclusions becomes
either false or vacuous.

### Proposition 1.1 (relative-escape necessity)

Assume the effectiveness hypotheses of the finite externalization proposition
in the foundations: histories, doctrines, events, certificates, supports, and
the validity relation are uniformly computably presented, and every realized
event and certificate is finite. Then:

1. every realized finite birth role has a finite external code;
2. an initial repair is, by definition, the freely universal solution in its
   declared category of solution-bearing extensions; and
3. after enlarging the metalanguage by a constructor implementing that repair,
   the same operation can be made available as a predeclared completion or
   lookup.

Consequently, no effective finite realization can establish absolute
nonsymbolicity, absence of every dictionary, or absence of every possible
free or completion presentation. Meaningful conclusions are instead relative:
nondefinability in an earlier object language, exclusion from a frozen
constructor closure, absence of a natural selector, or a lower bound in a
declared observer and cost class.

#### Proof

Item 1 is the finite externalization proposition. Item 2 is the universal
property of the initial object in the declared extension category. For item 3,
one may conservatively enlarge the external metalanguage by a symbol and
interpreter for the finitely coded operation. This does **not** prove that an
appropriate reflector, compiler, or object-language term already existed in
the original doctrine; it proves only that banning all such descriptions in
every possible ambient language is not a coherent target. \(\square\)

Two qualifications are load-bearing.

- Calling an initial object "free" in its solution category does not show that
  it is induced by an old generator/relation adjunction. The latter is the
  substantive collapse test.
- Adding an ad hoc completion to a larger metalanguage does not exhibit a
  natural reflector in the old category. The theorem must freeze which
  reflectors and parameterized completion formers count as old.

## 2. Freeze the comparison object first

Fix, before selecting a separating family, the datum

\[
\Xi_0=
(\mathcal L_0,\mathsf H_0,\mathsf{Pres},\simeq_{\rm st},
\simeq_{\rm gen},\mathscr A,\mathscr K,\mathscr R,\mathscr I,
\mathscr D,\mathcal O,\operatorname{cost}).
\tag{1}
\]

Here:

- \(\mathcal L_0\) is the old object doctrine and active formation doctrine;
- \(\mathsf H_0\) is the allowed category of finite causal histories;
- \(\mathsf{Pres}\), \(\simeq_{\rm st}\), and
  \(\simeq_{\rm gen}\) declare harmless recoding, semantic stutter, and
  process-level equivalence;
- \(\mathscr A\) is the class of old generator/relation adjunctions;
- \(\mathscr K\) is the allowed Cauchy or Karoubi closure;
- \(\mathscr R\) is the class of old reflectors, localizations,
  sheafifications, and parameterized completion formers;
- \(\mathscr I\) is the fixed family of admissible cell boundaries;
- \(\mathscr D\) is the class of old dictionaries, interpreters, and
  compilers;
- \(\mathcal O\) specifies exactly what an old observer may read; and
- \(\operatorname{cost}\) charges descriptions, compilation, persistent
  state, execution, replay, and support according to a declared model.

The choices in (1) are part of the theorem statement. Changing them after the
candidate family is known invalidates the comparison.

### 2.1 Composite old closure

For a finite bound \(B\), let

\[
\operatorname{OldSat}_B(\Xi_0)
\tag{2}
\]

be the least class of old role-producing constructions containing identities
and every instance of \(\mathscr A,\mathscr K,\mathscr R,\mathscr I\), and
\(\mathscr D\) of total cost at most \(B\), and closed under:

1. composition and finite zigzags;
2. old equivalence and retract;
3. admissible base change;
4. transport along presentation and stutter equivalence;
5. pseudonatural assembly over histories; and
6. every combination of the preceding operations whose complete compiler and
   execution cost remains at most \(B\).

Write \(\operatorname{OldSat}_{\infty}\) for the corresponding unbounded old
closure. The bounded and unbounded versions answer different questions:

- exclusion only from \(\operatorname{OldSat}_B\) can establish a Level-B or
  architectural complexity theorem;
- a Level-C claim requires exclusion from
  \(\operatorname{OldSat}_{\infty}\), a genuine change of formation doctrine,
  and failure of conservative translation back to \(\mathcal L_0\).

The union in (2) is essential. The coupled finite object \(Q\), for example,
is simultaneously a quotient presentation, a split idempotent, an orbit
reflection, and a quotient-cell pushout. Its action and coequalizer doctrine
are also predeclared. Passing one collapse test would therefore say little.

### 2.2 Role-valued collapse

Localize histories by the declared presentation and stutter equivalences. Let

\[
O:\mathsf H_0/(\mathsf{Pres},\simeq_{\rm st})\longrightarrow\mathcal E_0
\]

be the permitted old observation, and let

\[
U:\mathsf H_0/(\mathsf{Pres},\simeq_{\rm st})
\longrightarrow\mathsf{BirthRole}
\]

carry a history to its alleged universal birth role. A **composite collapse
witness** is a construction \(C\in\operatorname{OldSat}_B(\Xi_0)\) and a
pseudonatural equivalence

\[
U\simeq_{\rm fut}J\circ C\circ O.
\tag{3}
\]

The subscript in (3) means that the comparison preserves:

- the map from the identified base doctrine;
- the generated extension problem;
- the initial or homotopy-initial solution;
- retained support and independence structure;
- every admissible future continuation; and
- the essential later use of the universal role.

An isomorphism between current endpoint objects is not enough. Genetic
bisimulation compares solution roles and future transition doctrines, not only
static carriers.

## 3. Six exact collapse modes

### 3.1 Generator/relation adjunction

The birth collapses algebraically if there is a fixed old formation monad and
an equivalence, natural in history,

\[
i_h:\mathcal L_h\longrightarrow
\mathcal L_h\langle G_h\mid R_h\rangle,
\tag{4}
\]

where (4) is the unit of an adjunction in \(\mathscr A\), and all later uses of
the alleged new former admit a conservative macro-elimination translation.

**Falsifier.** Exhibit \(G_h\), \(R_h\), the unit comparison, and the
future-preserving elimination. A different printed name or a retained event
label does not defeat this collapse.

**Required escape invariant.** The universal role lies outside the essential
image of every allowed old algebraic adjunction, including their composite
closure. For a strict Level-C claim this must hold without a cost bound.

### 3.2 Cauchy or Karoubi completion

The birth collapses to idempotent splitting when old data contain

\[
e:Y\to Y,\qquad e^2=e,
\]

and

\[
Y\xrightarrow{p}Q\xrightarrow{j}Y,
\qquad pj=1_Q,\quad jp=e,
\tag{5}
\]

with the alleged birth and its future action transported through (5).

**Falsifier.** Split the idempotent in the old Cauchy completion and construct
the role- and future-preserving comparison (3).

**Qualification.** An endpoint retract trivializes the birth component only.
The entire event is a semantic stutter only if the full future transition
doctrines are equivalent while retaining identified questions, capabilities,
and birth roles. This is the distinction between universal-birth triviality
and semantic stutter in the foundations.

### 3.3 Reflective, sheaf, or localization completion

The birth collapses reflectively if an allowed old parameterized adjunction

\[
L_\lambda:\mathcal C\rightleftarrows\mathcal D_\lambda:V_\lambda
\tag{6}
\]

and old-definable \(\lambda_h,X_h\) recover it as the unit
\(X_h\to V_{\lambda_h}L_{\lambda_h}X_h\), with the comparison extending to
future roles.

This includes a history choosing a site, topology, local-object class, weak
equivalence class, or group action when the generic operation "sheafify or
localize at the chosen parameter" is already in \(\mathscr R\). The orbit
quotient in the coupled calibration is of this form once the action has been
declared.

**Falsifier.** Give the fixed reflector or parameterized completion former,
the old classifying parameter, and a pseudonatural comparison of units and
later descent.

**Required escape invariant.** The history must change more than the parameter
fed to an old reflector. Its role must be absent from the declared composite
reflective closure.

### 3.4 Fixed-doctrine cell attachment

For the fixed boundary family \(\mathscr I\), the birth collapses cellularly
when

\[
i_h\in\operatorname{cell}(\mathscr I),
\tag{7}
\]

including allowed pushouts, composites, transfinite composites, and retracts.
History-dependent choice of an attaching map inside a fixed cellular doctrine
does not create a new attachment former.

**Falsifier.** Produce the natural cell decomposition and transport the later
universal role through it.

**Required escape invariant.** A Level-C candidate must require an admissible
boundary or attachment principle outside the old weak saturation. An
unbounded old cell decomposition defeats Level C even if it leaves a bounded
architectural separation intact.

### 3.5 Predeclared dictionary or compiler

The birth collapses to lookup when there are an old dictionary \(D\), an old
evaluator, and a natural classifier

\[
\chi_h:O(h)\longrightarrow D
\tag{8}
\]

whose selected entry reconstructs the universal role and all admissible
future continuation.

**Falsifier.** Supply a bounded old-language compiler or evaluator realizing
(3). Recovering only an extensionally equal endpoint is insufficient, but a
generic interpreter that preserves the role is decisive.

**Required escape invariant.** The useful effective claim is that no natural
or bounded compiler in \(\mathscr D\) reconstructs the role. Absolute absence
of an external dictionary contradicts finite externalization.

### 3.6 Hidden stage counter

The birth contains a hidden clock when there are a rank \(c(h)\) and a
pretabulated family \(\Delta_m\) such that

\[
U(h)\simeq\Delta_{c(h)},
\tag{9}
\]

and semantically irrelevant idle insertion, padding, scheduler linearization,
or proof depth can alter the selected entry.

**Falsifier.** Find presentation- or stutter-equivalent histories with
inequivalent problems or birth roles. Additional tests insert irrelevant
independent events, thin redundant support, change proof presentation, and
subdivide causal paths.

**Qualification.** External-counter freedom in the foundations still permits
dependence on the intrinsic depth of normalized support. A stronger depth-free
claim must prove invariance under an explicitly declared class of
depth-changing semantic refinements. Even intrinsic depth can feed a hidden
dictionary, so clock and lookup tests overlap.

## 4. Minimum theorem package for a genuine family

Let \((h_n)_{n\geq 1}\) be finite histories, \(\mathcal P_n\) their
history-derived extension problems, and

\[
i_n:\mathcal L_{h_n}\longrightarrow\mathcal L_{h_n+b_n}
\]

their alleged universal births. The following package is the minimum serious
target.

### 4.1 Frozen quantifiers

The datum \(\Xi_0\), family sampling rule, observer order, and cost model are
fixed before selecting witnesses. In particular, the theorem must say whether
the observer is chosen before or after the active support is known and whether
it may replay the causal transcript.

### 4.2 Endogenous problem generation

The map \(h_n\mapsto\mathcal P_n\) is support-local and pseudonatural under
causal isomorphism, presentation equivalence, semantic stutter, and admissible
independent base change. A supplied address, stage number, or preselected
future branch is not allowed to carry the invariant.

### 4.3 Universal birth

The pair \((i_n,\eta_n)\) is initial or homotopy-initial in the declared
category \(\mathsf{Ext}(h_n,\mathcal P_n)\). The theorem identifies the proof
calculus and gives finite certificates whose soundness can in principle be
checked independently. Object-level universality in a familiar ambient
category is not automatically doctrine-extension initiality.

### 4.4 Composite obstruction invariant

Define a role-valued invariant \(\Omega\) such that:

1. \(\Omega\) is invariant under presentation, stutter, and genetic
   equivalence;
2. every operation in the relevant old composite closure preserves a declared
   zero set or bound for \(\Omega\); and
3. the candidate birth violates that zero set or exceeds the bound.

The strongest finite form also supplies **birth-fiber multiplicity**: two
histories with the same allowed old observation have inequivalent universal
future roles.

### 4.5 Family separation and cost

A bounded architectural theorem should have explicit quantifier order, for
example

\[
\forall B<\infty\;\exists n\;\forall
C\in\operatorname{OldSat}_B(\Xi_0),
\qquad
C(O(h_n))\not\simeq_{\rm fut}U(h_n),
\tag{10}
\]

while the causal realization has a stated cost in the same model. Compiler
description, compilation, persistent state, execution, replay, precision, and
support cannot be moved outside the accounting after the fact.

For Level C, replace bounded exclusion in (10) by a structural exclusion from
\(\operatorname{OldSat}_{\infty}\), prove that a new formation rule is
admitted, and rule out conservative translation into \(\mathcal L_0\).

### 4.6 Essential future use

There is a later valid history \(k_n\supseteq h_n+b_n\) and judgment \(J_n\)
such that

\[
k_n\vdash J_n,
\qquad
k_n-b_n\not\vdash J_n,
\tag{11}
\]

where \(k_n-b_n\) is a separately validated null-event replay. Every
derivation of \(J_n\) must use the retained universal formation role—not only
the answer, printed symbol, endpoint object, or a freely adjoined old
generator.

### 4.7 Honest conclusion

Every theorem states which boundary it has crossed:

- **Level A:** an old question or constructor instance was selected;
- **Level B:** a new object, predicate, representation, or probe was generated
  inside a fixed metadoctrine, possibly with a strict old-observer lower bound;
- **Level C:** the active formation doctrine changed in a way not induced by
  the entire frozen old closure.

Finite external simulation is compatible with relative Level B or C, but it
forbids rhetoric of absolute nonrepresentability.

## 5. Exact active-subspace cover theorem

The active-cover proposal has a clean exact core. It should be retained, but
classified correctly.

Let \(K\) be a connected finite CW complex with

\[
V=H^1(K;\mathbf F_2),\qquad \dim V=n,
\]

and let \(S\leq V\) have dimension \(r\). Define the evaluation homomorphism

\[
\rho_S:\pi_1K\longrightarrow S^*,
\qquad
\rho_S(g)(\chi)=\chi(g),
\tag{12}
\]

and let \(p_S:K_S\to K\) be the based connected cover corresponding to
\(H_S=\ker\rho_S\).

### Proposition 5.1 (coarsest exact sign-trivializing cover)

The cover \(p_S\) has the following properties.

1. The map \(\rho_S\) is surjective. Hence \(p_S\) is a connected regular
   \(S^*\)-cover of degree \(2^r\).
2. Pullback kills exactly the active subspace:
   \[
   \ker\bigl(p_S^*:H^1(K;\mathbf F_2)\to
   H^1(K_S;\mathbf F_2)\bigr)=S.
   \tag{13}
   \]
3. If \(L_\chi\) is the real sign local system classified by
   \(\chi\in V\), then
   \[
   H^0_\nabla(K_S;p_S^*L_\chi)\cong
   \begin{cases}
   \mathbf R,&\chi\in S,\\
   0,&\chi\notin S.
   \end{cases}
   \tag{14}
   \]
   After fixing a base-fiber unit, the nonzero unit parallel section is unique;
   without that choice the two unit sections form a sign torsor.
4. For every based connected cover \(q:Y\to K\) trivializing all characters
   in \(S\), there is a unique based map \(Y\to K_S\) over \(K\). Thus
   \(K_S\) is the coarsest connected simultaneous trivializer.
5. Every connected cover trivializing a \(t\)-dimensional subspace
   \(T\leq V\) has degree at least \(2^t\). The full sign-trivializing cover
   has degree \(2^n\), while the active cover has degree \(2^r\).

#### Proof

The inclusion \(S\hookrightarrow V\cong H_1(K;\mathbf F_2)^*\) dualizes,
in finite dimensions, to a surjection
\(H_1(K;\mathbf F_2)\twoheadrightarrow S^*\), giving item 1. A character
\(\chi\in V\) pulls back trivially exactly when it vanishes on
\(H_S\), equivalently when it factors through \(\rho_S\). The characters of
\(S^*\) form \(S^{**}\cong S\), proving (13). Equation (14) is the fixed-vector
calculation for a one-dimensional sign representation restricted to
\(H_S\).

If \(q\) trivializes \(S\), then
\(q_*\pi_1Y\leq H_S\), so the based covering-space lifting criterion gives the
unique map \(Y\to K_S\). Finally,
\([\pi_1K:H_S]=|S^*|=2^r\), and every refining subgroup has at least this
index. \(\square\)

The zero section exists for every real line bundle, so "actual section" in
this proposition must mean a nonzero parallel, or equivalently trivializing,
section. It must not mean mere existence of the zero section.

### 5.1 Ordinary completion classification

The cover is the standard pullback

\[
K_S\simeq K\times_{B(S^*)}E(S^*)
\simeq\operatorname{hofib}\bigl(K\to B(S^*)\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]

Therefore:

- if the old doctrine already contains classified covers, kernels, and
  pullbacks or homotopy fibers, \((K,S)\mapsto K_S\) is Level A;
- on a finite graph or CW presentation, it is also representable by ordinary
  lifted cells and relations, so it does not establish Level C; and
- it can support a Level-B architectural theorem only relative to a stricter
  fixed observer and timing comparison.

Actual local-system sections strengthen the semantic interpretation of (15),
but do not change its completion class.

### 5.2 Exact finite-state statement

Fix a \(d\)-dimensional query subspace \(T\leq V\). Consider deterministic
online observers which:

1. update a finite internal state under every allowed path transport;
2. must answer every character query \(\chi\in T\) exactly after traversal;
3. have no later access to the raw path transcript; and
4. operate on a path system whose evaluation image in \(T^*\) is all of
   \(T^*\).

Then exactly \(2^d\) distinguishable transport states are necessary and
sufficient. Sufficiency uses the syndrome in \(T^*\). For necessity, two
distinct syndromes differ on some \(\chi\in T\), so the required outputs
distinguish them. In memory units this is a lower bound of \(d\) bits, not
\(2^d\) bits.

Consequently, the restricted comparison is

\[
2^n\text{ states }(n\text{ bits})
\quad\text{versus}\quad
2^r\text{ states }(r\text{ bits}).
\tag{16}
\]

This is a finite-state transport theorem, not an unrestricted program-feature,
description-length, neural-width, or real-parameter lower bound.

### 5.3 Timing and compiler kill tests

The architectural interpretation fails or changes scope under any of the
following conditions.

1. **Same active input.** If the old observer receives \(S\) before traversal,
   a uniform compiler also maintains the \(r\)-bit syndrome. The active cover
   has no advantage over that observer.
2. **Symbolic state.** The \(2^r\) sheets have an \(r\)-bit symbolic encoding.
   Sheet count is not exponential description size or memory in bits.
3. **Aggregate primitive.** A vector-valued period primitive can return a
   basis of all active evaluations at once. Counting all \(2^r\) characters as
   independent features is invalid because \(r\) basis characters generate
   them.
4. **Single known query.** One character known before traversal requires only
   two states. Simultaneous readiness for the whole query subspace is
   load-bearing.
5. **Free replay or external history.** If the raw path remains available, an
   observer can recompute active parities later. Transcript storage, replay
   time, and random access must be charged.
6. **Nonuniform compiler.** A separately supplied program or table for each
   \(S\) defeats a uniform claim unless its description and compilation cost
   are included.
7. **Unbounded precision.** A single arbitrary-precision real can encode every
   sheet. Dimension or width lower bounds require finite-alphabet, precision,
   robustness, or information constraints.
8. **Restricted reachability.** If executable paths realize only a proper
   subspace of \(S^*\), replace \(r\) by the rank of the reachable evaluation
   image.
9. **Uncharged discovery.** Computing \(S\), choosing a basis, and compiling
   transition updates are part of the active method's cost.
10. **Late activation.** If \(S\subsetneq T\), an \(S^*\)-sheet has
    \(2^{\dim T-\dim S}\) lifts to the \(T^*\)-cover and no canonical lift.
    A compressed active state cannot reconstruct newly activated characters'
    past transport without retaining the missing coordinates, retaining or
    replaying the path, or declaring that the new semantics begins only at
    birth.

The strongest defensible Level-B statement therefore compares:

- an exact, context-blind observer committed before the active subspace is
  known and required to answer every later character without replay; with
- an adaptive observer that receives \(S\) before the transport interval to
  which the active semantics applies.

That comparison may be useful, but the differing information timing must be
printed in the theorem rather than hidden in the implementation.

## 6. Provisional escape: a history-derived guarded final-coalgebra former

The active cover does not approach Level C. A better hostile calibration is a
repair whose universal role is right-universal and future-parametric rather
than another finite quotient or cover.

### 6.1 Frozen old doctrine for the experiment

Let \(\mathcal L_0\) be a positive finitary doctrine with inductive algebraic
formation but without:

- greatest fixed-point or final-coalgebra formers;
- a generic coiterator;
- a duality that defines greatest fixed points from old least fixed points;
- pro-objects, inverse-limit completion, or final-sequence completion;
- infinitary or cyclic cells capable of installing coinduction; or
- a dictionary interpreter for arbitrary \(\nu P\).

The declared old algebraic, Karoubi, reflective, cellular, and dictionary
closures must additionally preserve a proved semantic invariant such as
bounded modal observation depth, well-founded generation, or absence of
stable coiteration.

These restrictions are not conclusions. They define the hostile test bed and
must be justified as a natural old class rather than chosen after seeing the
candidate.

### 6.2 Proposed history-derived problem

From the normalized causal support of a certified continuation defect,
construct a finite guarded polynomial or container functor

\[
P_h:\mathcal C_h\longrightarrow\mathcal C_h.
\tag{17}
\]

The proposed birth adjoins a local former \(\nu P_h\), a destructor

\[
\mathsf{out}_h:\nu P_h\longrightarrow P_h(\nu P_h),
\tag{18}
\]

and a stable coiteration principle: every present or future coalgebra
\(c:C\to P_hC\) admits a unique behavior map

\[
\mathsf{coit}(c):C\longrightarrow\nu P_h
\tag{19}
\]

commuting with the coalgebra structures. The extension problem is not merely
to name one final carrier. It is to admit the former and its universal action
on coalgebras that were not known when the birth occurred.

The candidate birth should be initial among doctrine extensions equipped with
a base-change-stable final \(P_h\)-coalgebra. A later event must introduce a
genuinely new coalgebra and essentially consume (19). That future-parametric
test is intended to rule out a precomputed endpoint or finite list of known
uses.

### 6.3 Candidate separating family

A provisional family compares a productive guarded loop with arbitrarily long
but terminating guarded chains. For every fixed finite modal depth \(B\), one
can choose a chain whose root agrees with the productive system on all tests
of depth at most \(B\). A greatest-fixed-point observation expressing
indefinite guarded continuation separates them.

To turn this familiar modal-depth fact into the required theorem, one must
prove:

1. every construction in the frozen
   \(\operatorname{OldSat}_B(\Xi_0)\) preserves the chosen bounded-depth or
   well-foundedness invariant;
2. the history-derived \(\nu P_h\) role violates it with controlled cost;
3. the construction of \(P_h\) descends through presentation, stutter, support
   thinning, and independent base change; and
4. the later use cannot be replaced at the allowed cost by finite unrolling.

Relative to that frozen doctrine, the candidate is designed to evade the first
five collapse modes:

- finite old generators and relations do not supply stable coiteration for
  arbitrary future coalgebras;
- splitting old idempotents does not create the finality role;
- the declared finitary reflectors preserve the separating invariant;
- the declared left-cell closure remains well-founded or finite-depth; and
- every bounded old dictionary has bounded modal depth or lacks the generic
  coiterator.

These are proof obligations, not established claims.

### 6.4 Seventh-collapse warning

The construction may simply belong to a seventh familiar class:

\[
\boxed{\text{cofree/final-coalgebra, guarded-recursive, or pro-completion}.}
\tag{20}
\]

It collapses immediately if the old doctrine contains any of the following:

1. a generic final-coalgebra or coiteration former;
2. greatest fixed points, including a definition by duality from old least
   fixed points;
3. a final-sequence, pro-object, inverse-limit, or guarded-recursion
   completion;
4. cyclic or infinitary cell machinery generating the same universal role;
5. a bounded interpreter for \(P\mapsto\nu P\);
6. a future-parametric encoding of coiteration in the old language; or
7. a bounded finite unrolling sufficient for every claimed later judgment.

The trigger also fails the clock test if \(P_h\) is chosen from the numerical
length or depth of the history rather than from a presentation- and
stutter-invariant structural continuation problem.

Accordingly, this candidate is not a novelty claim. Its value is adversarial:
it moves the next experiment away from another quotient, cover, or left-cell
repair, and it forces the collapse catalog to include right-universal and
limit-like completions.

## 7. Decision rule for the next construction

The next family should not be called Level C unless all of the following have
been proved together:

1. the old datum \(\Xi_0\) was frozen independently of the witness family;
2. the generated problem and universal role descend through the required
   presentation, stutter, support, and independence equivalences;
3. one role-valued invariant excludes the entire composite old closure rather
   than one construction class at a time;
4. the quantifier order and full cost model survive symbolic compilers,
   aggregate observers, replay, precision, and nonuniform advice;
5. a separately valid future ablation establishes essential use of the
   retained universal role; and
6. the candidate has been tested against newly identified completion routes,
   including coinductive and pro-completions.

Until then, the mathematically justified labels are narrower and still useful:

- the active-subspace cover is an exact Level-A construction and a conditional
  Level-B finite-state calibration;
- the guarded final-coalgebra former is a provisional relative escape
  experiment; and
- the existence of either construction is not evidence of absolute
  nonsymbolicity or a new foundational field.
