# Genesis Free Higher Attachment: a nonzero Postnikov obstruction

## Status: conditional higher-representer theorem

The gauge-complete successor produced an exact empty strict naturality
constraint.  Its unique `H`-`V` route comparison `R` fails to commute with the
declared third continuation `W` by the nonidentity diagonal deck element

\[
WR=TRW,
\qquad
T^2=1.
\tag{1}
\]

This note performs the next canonical experiment.  It declares one new
constructor:

> When a strict naturality problem has a certified boundary defect `T`, attach
> a higher filler freely, with no relations beyond the crossed-module laws and
> the equation that its boundary is `T`.

Relative to that constructor, the result is exact.  The failed naturality
square generates a dihedral subgroup of the raw permutation semantics; the
declared weak category promotes that subgroup to 1-transport.  Its universal
higher filler is an integral orbit module, and the resulting connected 2-type
has a nonzero Postnikov class.  Unlike the independent control cocycle in the
preceding experiment, this class is derived from the executable defect and the
universal attachment; it is not supplied as an associator table.

The executable companion is
[`genesis-free-crossed-module.mjs`](genesis-free-crossed-module.mjs).

The categorical promotion remains declared.  In particular, `R` is not a
strict morphism of the `W`-extended transition system.  The theorem does not
show that raw route comparisons must become 1-cells, that crossed modules are
the unique possible semantics of genesis, or that the system internally
decides to attach this filler.

## 1. The naturality square already contains `D8`

In the 32-state object, `W` and `R` are involutions and their commutator is
`T`.  Set

\[
p=WR.
\tag{2}
\]

Because the generators are involutions,

\[
p^2=WRWR=[W,R]=T.
\tag{3}
\]

The defect is nonidentity and has order two, so `p` has order four.  Moreover,

\[
WpW=RW=p^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently the local raw-permutation naturality group is

\[
\boxed{
P_0=\langle W,R\rangle
\cong
D_8
=
\langle p,W\mid p^4=W^2=1,\;WpW=p^{-1}\rangle,
}
\tag{5}
\]

and

\[
T=p^2\in Z(P_0).
\tag{6}
\]

This is important: the order-four/inversion pattern is not chosen from an
unrelated model.  It is generated by the two sides of the failed naturality
square.

The genuine gauge lift `G` commutes with both `W` and `R`, hence fixes this
entire local sector.  The preceding gauge-complete certificate identifies the
full transition-and-gauge group

\[
P_{\rm full}=\langle H,V,W,G\rangle
\cong UT_4(\mathbb F_2)\times C_2
\tag{7}
\]

also contains `P_0`, has order 128, and has the same central involution `T`.

## 2. The declared higher universal property

Fix a group `P` and a central involution `T` in `P`.  Consider triples

\[
(\partial:N\to P,n),
\tag{8}
\]

where `partial` is a crossed `P`-module and

\[
\partial n=T.
\tag{9}
\]

A morphism preserves the `P`-action, the boundary, and the distinguished
filler.  The desired higher attachment is the initial object in this
category: the free crossed `P`-module on one filler with boundary `T`.

Write

\[
Q=P/\langle T\rangle.
\tag{10}
\]

Let

\[
M=\mathbb Z[Q]
\tag{11}
\]

be the integral permutation module with basis elements `[q]`.  The `P`-action
factors through left translation by `Q`.  For

\[
m=\sum_{q\in Q}n_q[q],
\]

let

\[
\epsilon(m)=\sum_q n_q
\tag{12}
\]

be its augmentation, and define

\[
\boxed{
\partial m=T^{\epsilon(m)\bmod2}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

### Theorem 1: central-involution free crossed module

The map (13), with the regular action above, is the free crossed `P`-module
on the basis element `[1]` with boundary `T`.

### Proof

The first crossed-module identity holds because `T` is central:

\[
\partial(g\cdot m)=\partial m=g(\partial m)g^{-1}.
\]

The element `T` acts trivially on `M`, since it is trivial in `Q`.  Therefore
the second Peiffer identity holds: the action of every boundary element on
`M` is trivial, and `M` is abelian.

Now take any target (8).  The Peiffer law gives

\[
T\cdot n=(\partial n)\cdot n=nnn^{-1}=n.
\tag{14}
\]

Hence

\[
[g\langle T\rangle]\longmapsto g\cdot n
\tag{15}
\]

is well-defined.  All such translates commute: each has boundary `T`, so
conjugation by one acts on another as `T`, which fixes it by (14).  Thus (15)
extends uniquely from the free abelian basis to a `P`-equivariant crossed-
module map `M -> N`, and it is the only map sending `[1]` to `n`.  This proves
initiality.  \(\square\)

The infinite integral module is not an arbitrary enlargement: it is the free
abelian group on the `Q`-orbit of one chosen filler, so its coefficients are
formal integer multiplicities.  Interpreting those multiplicities as
observables or histories would require an additional semantic theorem.  Every
element has finite support and exact integer arithmetic.

## 3. The generated 2-type

For the connected 2-type represented by (13),

\[
\boxed{
\pi_1=P/\operatorname{im}\partial=Q,
}
\tag{16}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\pi_2=A_P
=
\ker\partial
=

}
\tag{17}
\]

The second group is the even-augmentation lattice, not merely the ordinary
augmentation ideal.  If `I_Q=ker epsilon`, then

\[
0\longrightarrow I_Q
\longrightarrow A_P
\xrightarrow{\epsilon}2\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{18}
\]

For the local group `P_0`,

\[
Q_0\cong C_2^2,
\qquad
M\cong\mathbb Z^4,
\tag{19}
\]

and `A_{P_0}` is an index-two rank-four lattice.  For the full group (7),

\[
|Q_{\rm full}|=64,
\tag{20}
\]

so its free filler module and its even-augmentation `pi_2` both have rank 64.

After adopting the declared free crossed-module constructor, this is the first
point in the construction where a finite strict state system yields an
infinite higher-comparison module.  It is still finitely presented and
computable; infinity alone is not non-soficity or noncomputability.

## 4. The Postnikov cocycle is derived, not declared

Choose a normalized section

\[
\sigma:Q\to P.
\tag{21}
\]

Because the kernel is `{1,T}`, there is a normalized factor set

\[
c:Q^2\to\mathbb F_2
\]

defined by

\[
\sigma(a)\sigma(b)
=T^{c(a,b)}\sigma(ab).
\tag{22}
\]

Lift this factor set into the universal filler module by

\[
F(a,b)=c(a,b)[1].
\tag{23}
\]

The chosen lift `F` need not itself be a 2-cocycle.  Its coboundary is

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(a,b,c)
=
a\cdot F(b,c)
+F(a,bc)
-F(ab,c)
-F(a,b).
}
\tag{24}
\]

Associativity in `P` implies that (24) has even augmentation, so it lands in
`A_P`.  Applying the group-cohomology differential once more gives

\[
\delta\kappa=0.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus `kappa` is the Postnikov 3-cocycle of the crossed module.  A different
section or a different lift changes its table by an `A_P`-valued coboundary,
not its cohomology class.  The crossed module is a strict 2-group, so `kappa`
is not an associativity failure inside that model.  It becomes an associator
cocycle only after passing to a chosen skeletal weak 2-group presentation.

There is a sharper structural identification.  Let `rho` be augmentation
modulo two, with `F_2` carrying the trivial `Q`-action.  Then

\[
0\longrightarrow A_P
\longrightarrow \mathbb Z[Q]
\xrightarrow{\epsilon\bmod2}\mathbb F_2
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The factor set `c` represents the central-extension class of

\[
1\longrightarrow\langle T\rangle
\longrightarrow P
\longrightarrow Q
\longrightarrow1.
\]

Equation (23) satisfies `rho F=c`, and equation (24) says exactly that

\[
\boxed{
[\kappa]=\beta([c]),
}
\]

where

\[
\beta:H^2(Q;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow H^3(Q;A_P)
\]

is the connecting homomorphism, up to the harmless global sign used by the
opposite connecting-map convention.  For finite `Q`, the regular integral
module `Z[Q]` is isomorphic to the module coinduced from the trivial subgroup,
so its positive-degree cohomology vanishes by Shapiro's lemma.  The long exact
sequence therefore makes `beta` an isomorphism.  Hence `[kappa]` is nonzero
exactly when the central extension is nonsplit, and every nonzero such class
has exact order two.  The universal primitive sequence transports the
extension class upward by one degree.

The logical order is now exact:

\[
\text{strict naturality defect }T
\longrightarrow
\text{free filler }F
\longrightarrow
\text{Postnikov residue }\kappa.
\tag{26}
\]

No independent 3-cochain is supplied as input at the last arrow.

## 5. A general nonvanishing theorem

The square root already found in (2)-(3) forces the class to survive.

### Theorem 2: order-four square-root criterion

Let `P` contain a central involution `T` and an element `p` of order four with

\[
p^2=T.
\tag{27}
\]

Then the Postnikov class of the free crossed-module attachment (13) is
nonzero:

\[
\boxed{
[\kappa]\ne0
\quad\text{in}\quad
H^3(Q;A_P).
}
\tag{28}
\]

### Proof

Let `x` be the image of `p` in `Q`; it generates a subgroup `C_2`.  Restrict
the section by

\[
\sigma(1)=1,
\qquad
\sigma(x)=p.
\]

Then `c(x,x)=1`, and (24) gives

\[
\kappa(x,x,x)=[x]-[1]=m.
\tag{29}
\]

The vector `m` lies in `ker(1+x)`.  If the restricted cocycle were a
coboundary, there would be an `a in A_P` satisfying

\[
m=(x-1)a.
\tag{30}
\]

Left translation by `x` partitions the basis `Q` into two-element orbits.
Equation (30) forces the coefficient difference to be odd on the orbit
`{1,x}` and even on every other orbit.  Modulo two, the sum of the two
coefficients on an orbit equals their difference.  Hence the total
augmentation of `a` would be odd, contradicting `a in A_P`.

Therefore the restriction is nonzero in

\[
H^3(C_2;A_P)
=
\ker(1+x)/(x-1)A_P,
\]

so the global class is nonzero.  The restricted class has order two, since

\[
2m=(x-1)([1]-[x])
\]

and `[1]-[x]` has augmentation zero.  \(\square\)

Applying the theorem with `p=WR` proves nonvanishing for the local naturality
group (5).  It also applies separately to any larger group in which the same
`T` remains central and `WR` remains an order-four square root, including (7).
The coefficient modules change with the ambient group, so these are analogous
classes related by restriction, not literally one unchanged class.

## 7. The universal doctrine is essential

The boundary certificate does not by itself force the free crossed module.
There is a valid thin alternative

\[
\langle T\rangle\hookrightarrow P
\]

with inclusion boundary and conjugation action.  Since `T` is central, it has
one nonidentity filler of boundary `T`, respects the full `P`-action, and has

\[
\pi_2=0.
\]

Its higher class is therefore zero.  This is an exact countermodel to the
claim that the finite state data and boundary `T` alone imply the Postnikov
obstruction.

There is also a valid pointed crossed module

\[
\mathbb Z\longrightarrow P,
\qquad
n\longmapsto T^{n\bmod2},
\]

with trivial `P`-action and distinguished filler `1`.  That filler has
infinite order.  Any initial pointed representer must map its universal filler
element to this element, so that universal element cannot have finite order.
Hence no crossed `P`-module with finite domain group can satisfy the universal
property of Theorem 1.

The exact separation is

\[
\boxed{
\text{state data and boundary }T
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{free crossed module},
}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
\text{boundary }T
+\text{ complete }P\text{-orbit}
+\text{ only Peiffer-forced relations}
\Longrightarrow
\mathbb Z[Q].
}
\]

The universal doctrine can now be stated as a question representability
theorem.  Let `XMod_P` be the category of crossed `P`-modules over `P`, and
define the filler-question functor

\[
\mathscr F_T(N)=\{n\in N:\partial_N n=T\}.
\]

### Theorem 3: unrestricted filler-question representability

The free attachment `F_P(T)=Z[Q]` represents the entire filler-question
functor:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{XMod}_P}(F_P(T),N)
\cong
\mathscr F_T(N),
}
\]

naturally in `N`.  The bijection evaluates a morphism at the distinguished
basis filler `[1]`; Theorem 1 constructs its unique inverse.  Consequently
every representing pair `(R,u)` is uniquely isomorphic to `(F_P(T),[1])` by
an isomorphism preserving the universal filler.

The thin inclusion does not represent this functor.  The integer target above
contains the filler `1`, but no homomorphism from the order-two thin generator
can send it to an element of infinite order.  Thinness therefore answers a
smaller question in which extra relations have already been imposed.

Thus the nonzero class tests a specific universal question doctrine.  A
cardinality-minimal filler collapses the higher residue.  For `P_0` and
`P_full`, the necessarily infinite representer of all pointed targets retains
the nonzero class.  The remaining foundational choice is sharper than “free
versus thin”: it is whether genesis promotes the failed square to the
unrestricted crossed-module filler question at all.  Once the doctrine
requires that unrestricted question to be represented, freeness follows by
representability rather than preference.

## 8. What changed conceptually

The previous abstract control exhibited a possible nonzero element of
`H^3(F_2^3;F_2)`, but the finite state process did not generate it.  The new
class has different coefficients and a different origin:

\[
[\kappa]\in H^3(Q;A_P).
\tag{34}
\]

It is forced after the universal higher attachment because three facts now
coexist:

1. strict naturality has the exact boundary `T`;
2. the boundary has an order-four square root `WR`; and
3. the filler is attached freely over the whole transport orbit.

The third obstruction is therefore not “noncommutativity implies an
associator.”  The literal reorder diagram still closes.  Rather:

> The free higher filler is strictly coherent as a crossed module, but an
> order-four lift of the quotient transport forces its 2-type to have a
> nontrivial Postnikov class.

Relative to the declared constructor, this is a precise candidate instance of
algebraogenesis.  The old state algebra did not contain the filler type.
Representing the failed question creates a new formal filler-orbit module, and
that module exposes a higher obstruction that was not meaningful at the
preceding level.

## 9. A cohomological boundary tower toward a Stokes-Hodge bridge

The construction has an internally linked boundary tower:

\[
T=\partial[1],
\qquad
\kappa=\delta F,
\qquad
\delta\kappa=0.
\tag{35}
\]

This is not yet a new Stokes or Hodge theorem.  It does, however, supply the
pieces that the earlier analogies lacked:

- a declared higher boundary operator whose value is fixed by the failed
  semantic square;
- a universal primitive module rather than a hand-selected coefficient
  space;
- a closed higher defect derived from local fillers;
- a nonzero subgroup restriction detected in cyclic cohomology; and
- an analogous full-group class, separately detected by the same cyclic
  restriction.

The next serious Stokes target is no longer “add a Laplacian to a suggestive
example.”  It is to derive a chain pairing and energy directly from the
filler-orbit module, then ask whether the nonzero class has a canonical
harmonic representative under the generated transport geometry.

## 10. Honest boundary

The theorem proves, relative to the stated higher constructor:

- an exact raw-permutation `D_8` naturality sector generated by `W` and `R`;
- the free crossed-module representer for a filler of `T`;
- the integral orbit module `Z[Q]` and its even-augmentation `pi_2`;
- a derived Postnikov cocycle;
- a nonzero 2-torsion, lattice-valued cohomology class detected on a generated
  `C_2`; and
- an analogous nonzero class for the full transition-and-gauge group, detected
  by the same cyclic restriction;
- a thin crossed-module control with the same boundary and zero `pi_2`; and
- the impossibility of a finite universal pointed filler.

It does not prove:

- that `W` is internally generated;
- that the genesis process internally selects crossed modules as its next
  semantic dimension;
- that the raw comparison `R` is already a 1-morphism before the declared
  weak categorical promotion;
- that every reasonable weak quotient preserves this class;
- that this crossed module is novel as an abstract mathematical object;
- that the class equals the earlier scalar control cocycle;
- non-soficity, noncomputability, or an infinite-depth theorem;
- a Hodge decomposition, Stokes theorem, or consequence for an open
  conjecture; or
- a new AI architecture.

The mathematical machinery is classical.  The novelty candidate is the
obstruction-triggered pipeline

\[
\text{finite executable semantics}
\to
\text{empty strict question type}
\to
\text{universal higher attachment}
\to
\text{new nonzero Postnikov class}.
\tag{36}
\]

The next theorem gate is to remove as much declaration as possible from the
third arrow: derive the crossed-module type former and unrestricted filler
question from the same locality and representability doctrine that forced the
earlier state-cover repairs, and test whether alternative weak semantic
categories produce an equivalent obstruction.
